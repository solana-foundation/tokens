`GET /v2/search`, `GET /v2/resolve` and `POST /v2/judge` share one pipeline. This page explains how it
judges; the routes are on [Search, Resolve & Judge](/v2/endpoints/search).

## The judgment pipeline

Candidates come from three sources, merged and deduped by mint: a live market-data provider for
long-tail coverage, the token index, and the curated asset registry — or, on `POST /v2/judge`, from
the caller (`caller`). Each candidate then passes through four stages:

1. **Intent classification** — is the query a mint address, a ticker or a name?
   (`interpretation.intent`). A mint address is a lookup, never fuzzy.
2. **Hard gates** — a policy decides which candidates are *suppressed*. Suppressed rows are
   returned under `suppressed[]` with the gate that fired; nothing is silently hidden.
3. **Weighted score** — the survivors are ranked (`score.total`, `score.components`).
4. **Explanations** — every ranked result carries `reasons`, `warnings` and `badges`; every
   suppressed row carries `suppressedBy`.

### Bring your own candidates

`POST /v2/judge` skips candidate gathering: you send the mints your own search returned (optionally
with the symbol, name and market fields it gave you) and the pipeline runs from the enrichment step.
Caller fields are claims with the same trust as a live-provider hit — the protected-symbol index,
dynamic dominance and attestations evaluate them against the registry and our snapshots, and our
market data wins wherever we have it. Nothing is looked up at the live provider, so the call draws
no provider budget. Without `q` no candidate can match-score and ranking is credibility alone.

### Provider deadline

The live provider gets a bounded slice of each uncached request (about 500 ms). When it is late,
the request is answered from the token index and the registry and `sources.provider` reads
`degraded`; the provider call is not cancelled, and its result still refreshes the candidate cache
after the response, so repeating the query inside the cache window returns the full set. A
`degraded` source on a small share of unique queries is therefore normal, not an outage: it means
the long tail was slow for that one request. A provider error inside the deadline is reported the
same way. Index and registry candidates are never affected.

## Policies and overrides

A policy is a declarative bundle of hard gates, score weights and a refusal bar. Presets:

| Gate | `strict` | `default` | `degen` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `minLiquidityUsd` | 10 000 | 1 000 | none |
| `requireMarketData` | true | false | false |
| `suppressImpersonation` | true | true | false |
| `minMarketScore` | none | none | none |
| `minAgeDays` | 1 | none | none |
| `requireRegistry` (`verifiedOnly`) | false | false | false |

`/v2/search` and `/v2/resolve` default to `default`; the curator search
`GET /v2/lists/search-tokens` defaults to `strict`.

Each gate can be overridden per request:

| Param | Accepts | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `minLiquidityUsd` | number ≥ 0, or `none` | Suppress below this liquidity (`gate_min_liquidity`). Registry-attested mints are exempt |
| `minAgeDays` | integer ≥ 0, or `none` | Suppress tokens younger than this (`gate_new_token`) |
| `minMarketScore` | integer 0–100, or `none` | Suppress tokens whose *known* score is below this (`gate_min_market_score`) |
| `requireMarketData` | `true`, `false` | Suppress tokens with no market snapshot (`gate_no_market_data`) |
| `suppressImpersonation` | `true`, `false` | Suppress likely impersonators of a protected symbol (`gate_impersonation`) |
| `verifiedOnly` | `true`, `false` | Suppress mints without a curated-registry variant (`gate_unverified`) |

`none`, `null` and `off` all disable a numeric gate; booleans also accept `1`/`0` and `yes`/`no`.
Out-of-range numbers and unknown `policy` values are `400`s; an invalid `policy` echoes the valid
ids in `details.policies`.

Overrides change only the gate you name; score weights and the refusal bar always come from the
preset. The response's `policy.gates` is the effective document and `policy.overrides` lists what
you changed, so any result is reproducible from its own envelope.

Two rules are **not** overridable under any preset:

- **Advisory gates**: mints carrying a `blocked` or `compromised` advisory are always suppressed
  (`gate_advisory_blocked`, `gate_advisory_compromised`). A `caution` advisory is a warning, not a
  gate. See [Advisories](/v1/concepts/advisories).
- **Tombstones**: mints editorially deleted from the corpus are always suppressed
  (`gate_tombstoned`).

Registry-attested assets bypass the liquidity gate: it exists to filter scams and unknowns, not to
hide verified assets that settle off-AMM (RFQ or primary-issuance tokenized stocks and funds). Thin
tradability on those shows as a `low_liquidity` warning instead.

## Reading the output

- **`reasons`** explain rank. **`warnings`** are caution signals on a token that is still ranked.
  **`suppressed[].suppressedBy`** names the gate that removed a candidate. All three vocabularies
  are enumerated below and are a stable contract: new codes may be added, existing ones are not
  renamed.
- **`suppressed`** rows are never truncated by `limit`. Loosen the gate, or switch to `degen`, to
  see them ranked instead.
- **`badges`** are free-form labels: `curated:<list>` for curated membership, `tier1`, `tier2`,
  `tier3`, `grade:A|B|C` (the same market grade v1 exposes, computed from the token's own
  liquidity, market cap, holders, volume and age) and `advisory:<status>`. A token with too little
  market data to score carries no `grade:` badge.
- **`claims.attestations`** are the evidence behind claim credibility: `curated_list`,
  `registry_variant`, `market_dominance`, `deep_liquidity`, `sustained_activity`,
  `established_age`, `broad_holder_base`, each with a human-readable `detail`.
- **`verified`** is `true` when a curated-registry variant exists for the mint.

## Explanation codes

### `reasons`

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `mint_match` | Query was this exact mint address |
| `exact_symbol_match` | Symbol equals the normalized query |
| `exact_name_match` | Name equals the normalized query |
| `symbol_prefix_match` | Symbol starts with the query |
| `name_match` | Name contains the query |
| `alias_match` | Reserved for alias matches; not emitted |
| `curated_list_member` | Mint is in one of the curated lists |
| `registry_variant` | Mint is a curated-registry variant |
| `market_leader` | Dominant market among the candidates claiming this symbol |
| `deep_liquidity` | Liquidity of at least $1M |
| `high_activity` | 24h volume of at least $250k |
| `established_token` | First seen at least 90 days ago |
| `strong_execution_quality` | Execution-quality score of at least 70 (tight, deep markets) |

### `warnings`

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `low_liquidity` | Liquidity below $10k, still ranked (the mint is registry-attested or the gate is off) |
| `no_market_data` | No market snapshot available |
| `possible_impersonation` | Claims a protected symbol, is not the protected holder, is not registry-attested, and is young or shallow |
| `symbol_collision` | Claims a protected symbol without being its holder, but is established enough not to look like an impersonator |
| `suspicious_characters` | Homoglyphs or invisible characters in the symbol or name |
| `new_token` | Younger than 7 days |
| `stale_data` | Market data older than 30 minutes |
| `high_bot_volume` | More than 60% of volume attributed to bots |
| `concentrated_holders` | Top-10 holders own more than 50% of supply (registry-attested mints exempt) |
| `unverified` | No curated-registry variant for this mint |
| `weak_market_score` | Known market score below 40 |
| `advisory_caution` | The mint carries a `caution` advisory; see [Advisories](/v1/concepts/advisories) |

### `suppressedBy`

| Code | Gate | Overridable |
|---|---|---|
| `gate_tombstoned` | Mint editorially deleted from the corpus | no |
| `gate_advisory_blocked` | Mint carries a `blocked` advisory | no |
| `gate_advisory_compromised` | Mint carries a `compromised` advisory | no |
| `gate_min_liquidity` | Below `minLiquidityUsd` (registry-attested mints exempt) | `minLiquidityUsd` |
| `gate_no_market_data` | No market snapshot and `requireMarketData` is on | `requireMarketData` |
| `gate_impersonation` | Likely impersonator of a protected symbol | `suppressImpersonation` |
| `gate_min_market_score` | Known market score below `minMarketScore` | `minMarketScore` |
| `gate_new_token` | Younger than `minAgeDays` | `minAgeDays` |
| `gate_unverified` | Not in the curated registry and `verifiedOnly=true` | `verifiedOnly` |

## Resolve: confidence or refusal

`GET /v2/resolve` runs the same pipeline over a fixed candidate window and answers with one of
three statuses, always with HTTP `200` when the request is valid:

- `resolved` — the best candidate clears the policy's refusal bar **and** beats the runner-up by a
  margin. `best.confidence` is 0–1; an exact mint paste is `1`.
- `ambiguous` — several credible claimers exist, typical for an unprotected symbol shared by
  unrelated tokens. Show `candidates` (at most 5, best first) and let the user pick.
- `no_confident_match` — nothing clears the bar. A confident-looking wrong answer is the worst
  possible output, so "I don't know" is a first-class response.

Each policy has its own refusal bar: `strict` refuses most readily, `degen` least.

## Cost

Unique queries can reach the live provider, so `/v2/search`, `/v2/resolve` and
`/v2/lists/search-tokens` share a per-key budget of 120 calls per 10 minutes by default. The three
routes also share a server-side cache of each normalized query's candidate set (about 45 seconds);
a hit is reported as `sources.cache: 'hit'` and is not charged. Only the candidate *set* is cached:
market data, advisories and tombstones are applied live on every call. See
[Provider lookup budget](/v2/errors-and-limits#provider-lookup-budget).
